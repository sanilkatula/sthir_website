Sthir Story

Hi, I’m Shalu Ahuja

Majorly diverging mind. Always thinking about the next possibility, another lens, another perspective, another rabbit hole.

A little about me

Indian mom and woman in technology, based in the National Capital Region, India (though you can ask me about south Indian and Indian Chinese food as well).

I believe settling in can feel like a spell and drifting through life would be dismaying to human consciousness.

So, I want to move through the world like a ripple in a still ocean, and still throw a thing or two with an aim to make a dent in the universe.

I believe choice is a muscle. It stays weak or drifts until practiced.

I teach making, owning and acting on choices when there isn’t an obvious “right” answer, choices that honour expression, relationship, and the complexity of being human.

Two moments

What stayed with me.

Two of my most memorable life moments, which made me see the limits of humans’ capacity to create their magic, are:

One frightened me to my core, like, what has fitting in and seeking validation done to me and my expression? And another gave me hope there is a way to physicalize the abstraction of hopes, dreams in all of us with right intention.

My people are the ones doing work that doesn’t fit in a box. It is too nuanced, too multidimensional, too much like a dreamy poem and too personal to them.

The story behind Sthir

How I got here.

I am also an engineer and business leader. Twenty-five years of building teams, products and systems, many of them firsts for the organizations I built them in.

Along the way I took myself into many creative, liberation-rooted spaces to learn Play with Presence, so I could live with joy and integrity without dumbing myself down. Then I started walking with young adults in the middle of identity shifts and career pivots, all under pressure to keep moving before they felt ready. They had the ambition. What they lacked was something to hold onto.

Sthir is that something. Play, reflection, explorations, prototypes, difficult conversations and community, practiced together, so my daughters, their friends and their friends’ friends can move forward with more steadiness and less noise.

Yes, I’m grateful for the technology and the engineering. And, I love play and human-centred design more. Laughing, crying, dreaming and making things with other people in the same room is the best part of my job, hands down!
