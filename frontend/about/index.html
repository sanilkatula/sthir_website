The story behind Sthir

Hi, I’m Shalu.

Engineer. Educator. Coach. Diverging Mind.

Always thinking about the next possibility, another lens, another perspective.

Indian mom and woman in technology, based in the National Capital Region, India (though you can ask me about South Indian and Indian Chinese food as well!).

To me, settling in feels like a spell and drifting through life feels like dismaying the human consciousness.

I teach making and owning choices when there isn’t an obvious “right” answer. Choices that honour your relationship with yourself, your expression and the complexity of being human.

The choices that brought me here ↓

Why this work is personal

Learning to choose for yourself.

Both my parents are educators, born to immigrants who lived through the aftereffects of Partition and colonial rule. They were the first in their families to go to university. I changed eight schools before Grade 12 because every time their income increased, they invested in better schooling and opportunities for me and my siblings.

I chose engineering because my grandmother’s friend’s grandson was becoming an engineer.

My first conscious choice was after my master’s in Computer Science: to work as an engineer rather than an educator.

My engineering career took me through twenty-five years of building teams, products and systems. I fancied myself an independent thinker, calling my own shots, not knowing that all the individual and systemic choices together shaped my worldview, my work view and my life view. No regrets, though I sometimes felt dumb for being so naive about the effects of those choices.

Along the way, I began questioning how much of my direction was truly mine. I wanted to learn from others without borrowing their voice or their version of success. I wanted to be somebody without becoming everybody.

If you’re choosing subjects, a course, a career or a problem to build a company around, you may recognise that feeling of making a choice yourself or making a choice by chance. You can have plenty of advice and still find it difficult to hear your own thinking. What interests you? Which expectations have you taken on? What could you try before committing?

Those questions led me into deeper exploration. That exploration, my study of human psychology and six years of coaching parents and young adults through identity transitions shape what I bring to Sthir: space to explore possibilities, find your own voice and practise choices you can own fiercely and unapologetically.

How this became Sthir

Two of my most memorable life moments made me see our capacity to create or kill our own magic:

“Have you written this message using GPT? It sounds like that.” ~ Bahaar, my daughter, responding to my heartfelt, encouraging nudge.

“I love the way your questions make my mind see. My work has become a living being, and I don’t fear the consequences of being a lone soul.” ~ Bryon, a colleague.

One frightened me to my core. It made me question my own expression: what the heck had fitting in and seeking validation done to my voice?

The other gave me hope that yes, with the right intentions and thoughtful questions, an abstract thought, a hope or a dream within us can find clear expression.

Making Choice a Play

Over the past six years, I’ve walked alongside young adults and their families through identity shifts and career choices. The young adults didn’t lack ambition. Sometimes they lacked their own reason to choose. Sometimes they feared the stakes of owning a choice. They needed space to explore and practise, with support.

Sthir is that space. It brings play, reflection, prototypes and peer-to-peer learning together, so they practise exploring possibilities, making choices and learning from what follows. I believe that, with enough practice, they can carry their choices with more steadiness, especially when weathering storms.

Yes, I’m grateful for technology and engineering, so I bring that structured, experimental thinking into the room. And, I love play and human-centred design more. Playing, imagining, creating and living big possibilities with other people in the same room is the best part of my job, hands down!
