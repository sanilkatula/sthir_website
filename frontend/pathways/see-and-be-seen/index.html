See, and Be Seen!

Ten minutes a day to see yourself and each other a little differently.

12 days from the day you subscribe toOn emailComplimentary · Open to all

Okay, first a little game. Have you ever…?

  • Wanted someone to notice the effort behind what you did?
  • Felt invisible when someone else got the credit?
  • Compared yourself with someone and quietly thought, “I could do that better”?
  • Been extra agreeable because you wanted to ask for something or receive a little appreciation?
  • Told a story in a way that made someone more likely to take your side?
  • Brushed off the very compliment you had secretly hoped to hear?

Pick one moment. Stay with it for a little while.

What did you say to yourself? What did you leave unsaid? What did you wish someone had noticed?

You don’t need to share this with me or give yourself a score. Let the moment become something you can explore.

We all want to be seen. Sometimes, we work very hard at it.

We become helpful, impressive, agreeable or indispensable. We hold back a question. We explain ourselves. We say “it’s nothing” when something mattered.

What are we hoping to receive? And what do we miss while watching for approval?

“In judging we keep ourselves from fresh moments of experience and rarely go beyond what we already know.”

Viola Spolin

What happens when we become less occupied with approval and more curious about the experience itself?

Transactional analysis offers another lens: strokes, the recognition we give and receive through greetings, appreciation, criticism and attention.

See, and Be Seen! is a complimentary 12-day email experience of journaling and play. Together, we explore how we see ourselves, interpret others’ intentions and give and receive recognition.

Every day, you receive a byte-sized teaching and a small invitation to play.

Through writing, observation and small playful experiments, you create space for yourself to:

  • Notice whose recognition you seek and what it means to you.
  • Separate what someone actually said from the intention you imagined.
  • Explore what you readily appreciate in others and struggle to acknowledge in yourself.
  • Practise asking for recognition, receiving it and offering it with presence.
  • Try a different response and notice what changes.

What might you leave with?

A clearer, somewhat objective view of your patterns around recognition, a little sthir between a situation and your response to it, and an action you want to try differently.

You don’t have to stop caring about you, or what people think. Still, You can become genuinely curious about how people, places, systems' responses shape your choices.

Twelve days. Ten minutes a day.
